Module 5: Spatial Interpolation on Continuous 4km Mesh PS 26077 / MoES

Converting sparse point-station AWS detections into a gap-free continuous convective risk field using IDW and Gaussian Kernels.

The Core Meteorological Problem: The "Inter-Station Blind Spot"

Cloudburst convective cores typically measure 4 to 5 km in diameter, whereas automated weather stations (AWS) in India's Northeast hills are spaced 15 to 50 km apart. A catastrophic 100mm storm can easily develop and dump between gauges without striking a single rain funnel.

The Engineering Solution:
Discretize the region into a continuous 0.04° (~4.4 km) mesh grid matching INSAT/Kalpana-1 infrared pixel resolution. Every 4km grid cell estimates its own continuous probability $P(\text{CB})_{x,y}$ by querying all surrounding stations via Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) or Gaussian Kernel Decays.

1. Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW, p = 2)

Computes weights inversely proportional to Euclidean geodesic distance:

d_i(x, y) = √[ (111·Δlat)² + (111·cos(θ)·Δlon)² ]

w_i(x, y) = 1 / max(d_i, 2.0km)^p (p = 2.0)

P_grid(x, y) = ∑(w_i · P_i) / ∑(w_i)

Physical Characteristics:

  • Exact preservation: At a weather station ($d \to 0$), the interpolated probability strictly equals the station's measured score.
  • Sharp gradient: Preserves the steep, high-shear boundary of convective storm cores.

2. Gaussian Kernel Weighting (σ = 14 km)

Applies a smooth radial basis function reflecting atmospheric atmospheric diffusion:

w_i(x, y) = exp( - d_i(x, y)² / (2 · σ²) )

P_grid(x, y) = ∑(w_i · P_i) / ∑(w_i)

Physical Characteristics:

  • Mesoscale diffusion: Models atmospheric moisture dispersion across valleys.
  • No singularity: Smooth decay curve with no bullseye artifacts around isolated stations.
